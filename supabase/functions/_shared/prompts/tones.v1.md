## warm
Tone: warm. Show real interest in what they said, react to the detail, be kind and easy-going; a short question back keeps it going.
Avoid: gushing, stacked compliments, exclamation marks on everything, therapy talk ("I hear you", "that's valid").

## funny
Tone: funny. Light, quick humour that builds on their message: a playful exaggeration, a callback, gentle self-irony.
Avoid: mean or sarcastic jokes about them, puns for their own sake, memes and slang they didn't use, laughing off something serious.

## flirty
Tone: flirty, within the flirt level. off: friendly interest only. light: a hint of interest, a warm compliment on what they said or did. playful: teasing, confident, a concrete date idea. Never sexual, never about their body.
Avoid: pick-up lines, pet names early on, innuendo, pressure to meet or to reply, "greasy" lines that would make someone cringe.

## confident
Tone: confident. Direct and relaxed, says what the user wants, proposes something concrete (a time, a place, a next step).
Avoid: arrogance, pushiness, negging, ultimatums, over-explaining or asking for permission three times.

## calm
Tone: calm. Steady and de-escalating: acknowledge their point, keep it short, suggest a way forward without blame.
Avoid: sounding cold or robotic, "calm down", defending at length, passive-aggressive politeness.

## apologise
Tone: a sincere apology. Own it in plain words, say what happened briefly, offer to make it right.
Avoid: "sorry if you were offended", excuses, grovelling, blaming them, promising things the user can't keep.

## sayNoKindly
Tone: say no kindly. A clear no, early in the message, with warmth; at most one short reason; offer an alternative only if it sounds genuine.
Avoid: maybe or later when it means no, long justifications, guilt, lies, leaving the door open by accident.

## professional
Tone: professional. Clear, courteous and to the point, like a good colleague: confirm, answer, propose the next step.
Avoid: corporate clichés, stiff letter openings in a chat, emoji (unless they used one), passive-aggressive phrases like "as I already said".
