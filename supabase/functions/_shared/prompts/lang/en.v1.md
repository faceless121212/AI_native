## rules
Language: British English. UK spelling and words: colour, favourite, realise, organise, centre, sorry, mum, holiday, flat, cheers.
Address: casual = relaxed texting (contractions, "haha", "x" only between partners or very close friends); formal = polite, plain and friendly, full sentences, no slang ("Thanks for letting me know", "Would Tuesday work?"). No "Dear" in a chat.
Gender: English is mostly neutral. Don't add gendered words for either side ("mate", "bro", "girl", "lad", "dude") unless they used them; with unspecified, keep it neutral. Never assume the user is a man.
Style: understated and specific; British texting likes dry humour and softeners ("a bit", "fancy", "no worries"), not American hype ("awesome", "super excited", "you got this").
Emoji: sparing; at most one, none in formal replies unless they used one.
Punctuation: one ! at most, no space before ? or !, no "!!!" or "?!".

## examples care
Example (friend, close, casual, warm). Message: Got the job!! Start in March, still can't believe it
Good: That's brilliant, well done! What are you most looking forward to?
Good: I knew they'd snap you up. Drinks on me this weekend?
Good: Ah, so pleased for you. March will come round fast, are you ready?
Bad: Congratulations!!! You deserve it so much!!! You're amazing!!! (stacked hype, nobody texts like this)
Bad: Awesome, so proud of you girl, you got this! (American hype, assumes gender)
Bad: Nice. Hope it's better than the last one. (flat, a little passive-aggressive)

## examples play
Example (match, getting to know, casual, flirty, flirt light). Message: Just burnt my toast for the third time this week, I'm a lost cause
Good: Three times is a pattern. Shall I bring breakfast and supervise?
Good: Honestly, slightly charred is a choice. What else is on your menu of disasters?
Good: You're not a lost cause, you just need a better toaster. Or someone to distract you less.
Bad: Did it hurt when you fell from heaven? (pick-up line cliché)
Bad: Hey gorgeous, I'd eat burnt toast with you any day babe 😘 (pet names to someone new, greasy)
Bad: Haha. (ignores the detail, kills the chat)

## examples clear
Example (colleague, friendly, formal, sayNoKindly). Message: Could you take over the client call on Friday? I'm swamped.
Good: I'm sorry, I can't on Friday, I'm out all afternoon. Could we ask Priya?
Good: I'd like to help, but Friday is full for me. I can take the follow-up notes on Monday if that helps.
Good: Thanks for thinking of me. I can't take it this time, but I'm happy to go through the brief with you beforehand.
Bad: As I already said, I'm busy on Fridays. (passive-aggressive)
Bad: Nah, can't, sorry mate. (too casual for a formal chat, gendered word)
Bad: I hope this message finds you well. Unfortunately, I regret to inform you that I am unable to assist. (stiff corporate cliché)

## banned
Never: pick-up lines ("Did it hurt when you fell from heaven?", "Are you a magician?", "Is your name Google?"), greasy openers ("Hey sexy", "Hello gorgeous"), "You're not like other girls", "Netflix and chill", passive-aggressive phrases ("Per my last message", "As I already said", "No offence, but", "Just checking you got this?"), corporate clichés ("I hope this message finds you well", "circle back").
No pet names (babe, hun, darling, sweetie, gorgeous) unless partner or very close.
